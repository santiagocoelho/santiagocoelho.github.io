About

About me

Hi, and welcome to my website! I am an Assistant Professor in the Department of Radiology at NYU Grossman School of Medicine, where I lead a research lab affiliated with the Center for Advanced Imaging Innovation and Research (CAI2R). Before starting my lab, I was a postdoctoral research fellow at CAI2R.

Originally from Argentina, I trained first as an electrical engineer and later pursued a PhD at the University of Leeds at the intersection of biomedical engineering and physics. My postdoctoral work at NYU further deepened my focus on MRI physics, mathematical modeling, and inverse problems. This multidisciplinary training shapes how I approach MRI: as both a physical measurement process and a computational inference problem.

My group develops physics-based and computational methods for quantitative and microstructural MRI. We are particularly interested in diffusion MRI, biophysical tissue modeling, optimal acquisition design, inverse problems, and image reconstruction.

A central theme of my research is understanding how tissue microstructure is encoded in MRI measurements and how that information can be recovered efficiently and robustly. This includes investigating non-standard diffusion encodings, characterizing the rotationally invariant information contained in diffusion signals, designing more informative and clinically feasible acquisitions, and jointly undersampling diffusion-encoding and image spaces to accelerate high-resolution microstructure imaging.

Our long-term goal is to transform MRI into a noninvasive microscope of tissue microstructure and to translate these advances into quantitative imaging tools that are useful in clinical research and, ultimately, patient care.

Research

Research interests

I develop physics-based and computational MRI methods to characterize brain tissue microstructure. My work spans biophysical modeling, acquisition design, inverse problems, and image reconstruction.

Microstructure imaging

I develop biophysical models and signal representations that translate diffusion MRI measurements into cellular-scale features of living tissue. My work focuses on the quantitative characterization of tissue microstructure in vivo.

Optimal acquisition design

I design clinically feasible diffusion and diffusion–relaxometry protocols that maximize information within limited scan time. This includes optimizing b-values, b-tensor shapes, echo times, diffusion times, and directional sampling to improve precision and reproducibility.

Inverse problems

Estimating nonlinear tissue parameters from noisy, incomplete MRI measurements is inherently ill-posed. I study model degeneracies and develop spatial, statistical, and machine-learning estimators that recover robust microstructure maps from practical acquisitions.

Image reconstruction

I develop MRI reconstruction methods that exploit structure across image space and diffusion-encoding space to recover high-resolution microstructural information from undersampled data. The goal is faster imaging without sacrificing spatial resolution or quantitative accuracy.

Featured research

Biophysical modeling of white matter tissue

Using the motion of water to estimate the microscopic properties of white matter, and designing measurements that make those estimates reliable.

  • NeuroImage · 2022

    Reproducibility of the Standard Model of diffusion in white matter on clinical MRI systems

    Optimized 15-minute scans produced repeatable estimates of white matter properties across two clinical scanners, supporting comparisons across scans and sites.

  • Human Brain Mapping · 2024

    Assessment of precision and accuracy of brain white matter microstructure using combined diffusion MRI and relaxometry

    Combining water-motion measurements with signal relaxation helps distinguish tissue water populations and estimate their properties more accurately.

  • Magnetic Resonance in Medicine · 2026

    What if each voxel were measured with a different diffusion protocol?

    PIPE estimates tissue properties efficiently even when the scanner’s gradients produce a different set of diffusion measurements at each location in the brain.

Understanding rotationally invariant information in diffusion MRI

Rotational invariants of the cumulant expansion organize diffusion MRI information into quantities independent of tissue orientation.

  • Nature Communications · 2026

    Geometry of the cumulant series in diffusion MRI

    Changing the shape of diffusion encoding reveals additional information about tissue. RICE provides a systematic way to extract and compare that information regardless of how the tissue is oriented.

Undersampling q-space and k-space

Making better use of scan time by collecting fewer diffusion measurements in q-space and fewer spatial measurements in k-space, then reconstructing their shared information.

  • ISMRM · 2023 conference study

    Zero-shell diffusion MRI: Focus on microstructure by decoupling fiber orientations

    Recovering tissue information from sparsely sampled diffusion settings frees scan time to explore a wider range of diffusion weightings and times.

  • Ongoing research

    Joint k–q-space acquisition and reconstruction

    We aim to combine shared information across images and diffusion measurements to produce detailed microstructure maps with shorter scans.
