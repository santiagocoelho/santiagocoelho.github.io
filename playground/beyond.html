Beyond the b-value

How far does water explore?

During the diffusion time, a water molecule typically moves only a few micrometres from where it started. A millimetre-wide voxel therefore collects signal from water exploring many different local environments.

If water mixes within each compartment and exchange between compartments is slow, we can describe each population by its own diffusion tensor. One voxel then contains a distribution of diffusion tensors. Compare two such distributions below. RICE paper ↗

VOXEL A

Dispersed orientations

Each compartment has the same elongated diffusion tensor, rotated in a different direction.

VOXEL B

Mixed diffusivities

Each compartment diffuses equally in all directions. Larger spheres represent higher diffusivity.

Why do A and B agree with linear encoding?

LTE measures the apparent diffusivity along one direction. In A it varies with compartment orientation. In B it varies with the size of the spherical tensor. At low b, the first two terms of the log signal measure the mean and variance of that apparent diffusivity.

LTE termVoxel AVoxel B
O(b) · Mean diffusivity (µm²/ms)0.800.80
O(b²) · Diffusivity variance ((µm²/ms)²)0.200.20

Both log signals start with the same slope and curvature: ln(S/S₀) = −bμ + b²σ²/2 + O(b³), with b in ms/µm². Here the entire LTE diffusivity distribution matches, so the curves overlap even beyond second order. Try planar or spherical encoding to separate them.

How the two voxels are matched

Both voxels are mixtures of non-exchanging Gaussian compartments. In A, the principal diffusivities are 1.8, 0.3 and 0.3 µm²/ms with uniformly distributed orientations. B samples the distribution of apparent diffusivities that LTE sees in A. This matches the entire LTE curve, including the mean diffusivity of 0.8 µm²/ms.

The signal is the average of exp(−B:D) across compartments. At fixed b, the encoding tensor assigns its weight to one axis (LTE), equally to two axes (PTE), or equally to three axes (STE). The drawings show representative diffusion tensors. Read about multidimensional encoding and RICE.
